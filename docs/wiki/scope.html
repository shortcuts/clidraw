Scope and design choices

clidraw takes its core model from excalidraw and tldraw. It does not match either one feature for feature.

clidraw is a general sketching canvas. It is a whiteboard, not a tool for one kind of diagram.

This page lists what clidraw does and does not do, and why some features are missing.

Covered

  • Any engineering diagram built from shapes, lines and labels, such as a flowchart, a sequence diagram or an architecture sketch.
  • Shapes: rectangle, diamond, slant, hexagon, cylinder and frame.
  • Lines and arrows that bind to shapes. A bound end follows its shape when the shape moves.
  • Frames that move their content together.
  • Marquee select (Select tool, or Shift+drag on empty canvas), multi-select drag, group and ungroup.
  • Align, distribute and z-order commands.
  • Snapping to shape edges and centers, and a grid.
  • Labels with line breaks, word wrap, bold and alignment.
  • Undo and redo, kept across sessions.
  • Find by label, a command palette and a full-screen minimap.
  • Auto layout, and Mermaid import.
  • A library of reusable snippets.
  • Shape links, exported to SVG and Mermaid.
  • Export to SVG, Mermaid and the clidraw save format.

Not covered

  • Images, freehand drawing, sticky notes, video and embeds.

Left out on purpose

  • Live collaboration and shared canvases. Each canvas is one local file, so there is nothing to sync.
  • Accounts and cloud storage. Your drawings stay on your disk, and you share them with any tool that copies files.

The same choice makes canvases easy to back up and review. See Where files live.
